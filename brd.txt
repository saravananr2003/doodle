Entity360 Business Requirements

Account and Contact Master Data Management

Version 4.2  |  October 2 2026  |  DRAFT for review

The solution will master Accounts and Contacts across first-party and third-party sources, publish explainable golden records, and classify each Account and Contact cluster as Customer or Prospect using source membership. It must operate against up to 1.5 billion resident source records while preserving identity, history, business relationships, and governed access.

The recommended approach combines configurable rules, machine learning, human stewardship, and distributed incremental processing. Account identity, site identity, commercial account identity, and person identity must remain distinct. Matching a person does not prove employment, and discovering a prospect does not establish permission to contact them.

1 Purpose and scope

This document defines the consolidated business requirements and measurable acceptance conditions for product planning, architecture, implementation, and user acceptance testing. Business sponsors, Sales Operations, Marketing, Account Services, data stewards, Data Engineering, security, and platform operations are the intended stakeholders. TAMR is the capability benchmark, rather than a mandated algorithm or a claim of feature or performance parity.

In scope are source onboarding, quality and standardization, deduplication, Account and Contact resolution, hierarchy and affiliation management, survivorship, enrichment, Customer and Prospect classification, stewardship, configuration, publishing, auditing, and enterprise operations. Transactional sales, service, and engagement data can supply governed activity and prioritization signals; the platform does not become the transaction system of record.

Out of scope are replacing CRM or ERP workflows, autonomous sales outreach, purchasing third-party datasets without approval, a full marketing campaign engine, and revenue recognition or financial consolidation. Consumer household mastering, products, and anonymous device identity are optional future domains. API-based source writeback and near-real-time ingestion are recommended additions and require explicit implementation scope and acceptance gates.

1 1 Confirmed initial rollout

Initial source data resides on local disk. An administrator configures the base directory globally; each source has incoming, processing and reject directories beneath it. Internal application calls are included. External-system APIs for search, matching, enrichment or Customer and Prospect lookup are deferred. Azure ADLS integration is outside the initial rollout. Endpoint specifications belong in the separate API Technical Design document.

ROW_CREATE_TIMESTAMP, ROW_UPDATE_TIMESTAMP and ROW_STATUS are required standard record columns. A DELETED source identity contributes no information to matching, survivorship or classification, including historical evidence. Remove its old records and derived artifacts, then reprocess the affected cluster. The 1.5B requirement remains a scale target requiring full-footprint validation; local-disk landing does not imply a single-disk resident corpus.

1 2 Requirement classification

Baseline requirements describe the retained business scope. Extensions reflect additions for Entity360. Recommendations are proposed design choices requiring review.

Priorities: P0 is initial rollout scope; P1 is a later release or separately approved scope; P2 is optional. Explicit rollout exclusions take precedence over priority labels.



Priorities express dependency and risk, not confirmed funding or delivery dates. Acceptance criteria below are proposed release gates. Targets inherited from the references are retained as planning targets and must be ratified against an approved workload and infrastructure budget.

2 Business outcomes and domain definitions

Outcome

Required business result

Trusted identity

Stable Account and Contact IDs reconcile source identities without merging distinct organizations, sites, or people.

Operational quality

Lower duplicate creation, measurable match accuracy, fewer manual corrections, and defensible source attribution.

Account coverage

Classify Account and Site clusters as Customers or Prospects; preserve corporate families and channel ownership.

Contact coverage

Identify existing and missing buyer roles, valid contact channels, and current affiliations.

Scale and reliability

Process incremental changes against 1.5B resident records with restartability, controlled costs, and auditable deletion.

Consumer adoption

Publish consistent current and historical views through governed APIs, warehouse datasets, and bulk exports.



2 1 Entity boundaries

Entity

Identity grain and relationships

Organization

A real legal or operating organization. Names, identifiers, and business characteristics are attached at the appropriate grain.

Account

A commercial relationship or source account such as SBA Master, bill-to, CRM account, or channel account. Several can relate to one organization without being collapsed.

Site

A physical business establishment with its own identity. Multiple businesses can occupy one address, and one business can occupy multiple suites or sites.

Contact

A natural person with a persistent identity independent of employer, email, or phone changes.

Affiliation

A dated Contact-to-organization, account, or site relationship with role, title, department, confidence, and evidence. Many-to-many is supported.

Contact point

A typed email, phone, fax, or address with validity dates, verification, ownership context, and permitted-use status.

Source record

A record identified by source system, entity type, source ID, and source version. Resident records are counted at this grain.

Golden record

The governed current view at a specified entity grain, with lineage to all contributing records and an effective-dated history.



A branch, franchise, parent, subsidiary, department, ship-to, and bill-to are relationships or separately mastered entities according to a configured grain. A shared ultimate parent, domain, telephone switchboard, industry code, or address is supporting evidence; none independently proves duplicate identity.

3 Source onboarding and canonical data

ING-01  Source registry

The solution shall manage first-party and third-party sources with owner, category, domain, lifecycle, effective dates, cadence, file pattern, header count, input, reject, and archive locations. Sources can be paused, disabled, and retired through governed configuration.

Acceptance: Only active and effective sources execute; disabling a source stops new ingestion without deleting its historical lineage.  [Baseline; P0]

ING-02  File and extract ingestion

The solution shall ingest CSV, JSON, and Parquet, including large partitioned files, and record source extraction time, delivery ID, checksum, schema version, and record counts. It shall support manual and scheduled delivery and concurrent sources.

Acceptance: All configured formats load; duplicate deliveries are recognized; file-level totals reconcile and successful-file disposition follows the approved retention policy.  [Baseline; P0]

ING-03  Validation and quarantine

Required fields shall vary by entity and source. Malformed, missing-key, invalid-type, and unmapped records shall be quarantined with reason, raw reference, and corrective action. Bad-record thresholds shall be configurable by source and severity.

Acceptance: The configured threshold fails or partially completes a delivery as defined; accepted, rejected, duplicate, and pending records reconcile without silent loss.  [Baseline; P0]

ING-04  Source mapping

Source fields shall map to canonical fields using transformations, data types, storage types and formats, one or multiple date formats, samples, and documentation. Mappings shall support active flags, repeating values, concatenation, and conditional transformations.

Acceptance: Preview shows raw and transformed values; required mappings are validated before activation; changes retain prior versions and actor.  [Baseline; P0]

ING-05  Canonical dictionary

Administrators shall configure attributes and groups, business definitions, display order, enabled status, golden inclusion, match relevance, deterministic or strong or supporting or enrichment roles, and repeating cardinality. Domain and identifier namespace shall be explicit.

Acceptance: Adding an attribute exposes it consistently in ingestion, rule configuration, lineage, authorized UI, and publishing without rebuilding existing records unnecessarily.  [Baseline; P0]

ING-06  Content duplicate suppression

The solution shall suppress unchanged payload reprocessing using a stable hash scoped to source identity and version, while retaining duplicate count, original record reference, and first and last seen times. Distinct source identities with equal values shall retain independent xrefs.

Acceptance: A replay creates no extra active record; identical data from two systems preserves both source IDs; changed payload is processed as an update rather than discarded.  [Baseline; P0]

SRC-01  First-party source coverage

The solution shall onboard configured CRM Accounts and Contacts, SBA Masters, bill-to and ship-to records, ecommerce and registration data, service records, and channel-specific data. First-party membership shall determine Customer classification; authoritative transactional references shall supply separate activity and ownership attributes without importing unrestricted transaction history.

Acceptance: A person and organization present in multiple channels are linked while commercial accounts, source ownership, and source IDs remain distinguishable.  [Extension; P0]

SRC-02  Third-party source coverage

The solution shall support licensed account, site, hierarchy, contact, industry, government, healthcare, education, and enrichment feeds. Example candidates include D&B, Data Axle, ZoomInfo, MDR, GovSpend, and Definitive Healthcare; actual availability and use are contract-dependent.

Acceptance: Each feed has a field contract, permitted purposes, effective dates, refresh cadence, and entitlement policy; no required feature presumes unavailable vendor fields.  [Extension; P0]

SRC-03  Updates and deletions

The solution shall process inserts, updates, deletes, reactivations, source-ID reuse, late arrivals, and full-snapshot reconciliation. Source presence, last seen, tombstone, and business lifecycle state shall be separate. Snapshot omissions count as deletions only under an approved completeness policy.

Acceptance: Out-of-order updates do not overwrite newer facts; confirmed deletions deactivate contributions and refresh affected goldens, relationships, indexes, and exports.  [Extension; P0]

SRC-04  Schema evolution and profiling

Onboarding shall profile distributions, missingness, uniqueness, key overlap, candidate volumes, and data drift. Additive fields can be proposed; breaking schema and datatype changes require impact review. Source IDs and postal codes shall preserve leading zeros.

Acceptance: A numeric-to-character change produces an alert and downstream compatibility assessment; invalid mappings remain quarantined until approved.  [Extension; P0]

SRC-05  Incremental ingestion

The solution shall use source version or watermark controls and idempotent delivery state to process deltas against resident history. Monthly vendor snapshots shall produce differences rather than forcing a full rematch of all records.

Acceptance: An unchanged monthly refresh causes no unnecessary golden version; changed attributes trigger only the affected processing scope.  [Extension; P0]

3 1 Local disk configuration and file operations

FILE-01  Global configuration page

An Administrator shall set and version an absolute base directory through a Global Configuration page. The page shall show validation status, revision, actor and effective time.

Acceptance: Valid authorized paths can be saved; unauthorized users cannot alter the base directory.  [Extension; P0]

FILE-02  Source directories

Each source shall configure its own incoming, processing and reject directories within the base directory. Paths shall resolve canonically and remain distinct across active sources.

Acceptance: Traversal, escaping symlinks, conflicting paths and missing access permissions are rejected.  [Extension; P0]

FILE-03  Configuration changes

Directory changes shall use impact preview and a new configuration revision. Active jobs shall retain their starting revision; changes shall not move active files silently.

Acceptance: A running delivery continues against its approved paths; new deliveries use the new revision only after validation.  [Extension; P0]

FILE-04  Safe file claiming

Processing shall recognize complete deliveries, claim each file once and move or stage it into the source processing directory with a delivery ledger.

Acceptance: Concurrent scanners cannot process the same delivery twice; an unfinished upload is not ingested.  [Extension; P0]

FILE-05  Rejections and reconciliation

Rejected records shall be placed in the source reject directory with reasons and delivery references. Accepted, rejected, unchanged and deferred counts shall reconcile.

Acceptance: Resubmission preserves the original rejection reference and creates no duplicate identity.  [Extension; P0]

FILE-06  Restart and disk health

File operations shall support checkpoints, controlled retries, disk capacity monitoring, access failures and stale delivery detection. Successful-file retention shall be defined before production.

Acceptance: Restarting after file claim or record load causes no logical loss or duplicate publication; low disk or permission errors are visible.  [Extension; P0]

3 2 Standard columns and deleted source identities

Column

Required semantics

ROW_CREATE_TIMESTAMP

UTC timestamp of initial logical-row creation in Entity360; preserved through updates to that row.

ROW_UPDATE_TIMESTAMP

UTC timestamp of the latest accepted row mutation, including deletion; source event ordering is stored separately.

ROW_STATUS

Required state including ACTIVE and DELETED. Additional values require explicit definitions; unknown or missing states are rejected or quarantined.



The three columns apply to current source and canonical records and the golden row contract. Entity360 may populate its own technical timestamps and ACTIVE state under a documented adapter contract; it shall never silently default a missing source deletion signal. Mapping must preserve source event timestamps separately from Entity360 row timestamps.

DEL-01  Immediate matching exclusion

When ROW_STATUS is DELETED, exclude the source identity and all its historical names, addresses, contact points, aliases and derived features before any subsequent match evaluation.

Acceptance: No current, historical, cached or indexed value from the identity appears as matching evidence.  [Extension; P0]

DEL-02  Purge old records

Remove all current and historical operational records and derived artifacts for the deleted source identity, including candidate entries, source assertions, match evidence and membership contributions.

Acceptance: Counts reconcile across operational stores, indexes and caches; a retained minimal tombstone contains no reusable deleted attributes.  [Extension; P0]

DEL-03  Cluster reprocessing

Remove related match edges and contributions and recompute the affected cluster with remaining nondeleted members. Re-evaluate possible splits, golden winners, hierarchy and affiliations.

Acceptance: A deleted bridge produces the correct separate components; attributes supported only by the deleted identity cannot survive.  [Extension; P0]

DEL-04  Classification and retirement

Recompute Customer or Prospect from remaining valid members. Retire an empty entity rather than publishing an empty Prospect.

Acceptance: Deleting the last first-party member changes Customer to Prospect if third-party members remain; deleting all members retires the entity.  [Extension; P0]

DEL-05  Scoped identity and replay protection

Scope deletion by source system, entity type and source ID, with namespaced key and epoch where needed. Older files, restores and unchanged replays cannot reinstate it.

Acceptance: The same literal ID in another source is unaffected; stale deliveries remain blocked.  [Extension; P0]

DEL-06  Correction propagation

Correct all internal views and configured downstream datasets after cluster reprocessing. Remove deleted-only facts from historical golden outputs as well as current outputs.

Acceptance: No internal history or export exposes the removed source attributes; consumers receive explicit corrections.  [Extension; P0]

DEL-07  Deletion audit and recovery

Retain minimal identity, deletion watermark, time, actor, outcome and processing counts. Purge or sanitize raw and reject copies carrying deleted record data; restoration reapplies deletion markers.

Acceptance: A recovery test cannot resurrect deleted evidence; no audit payload or training snapshot reintroduces the removed attributes.  [Extension; P0]

4 Standardization and quality

DQ-01  Account normalization

The solution shall preserve original and standardized legal and trading names; normalize case, spacing, punctuation, legal suffixes, aliases, address abbreviations, directionals, domains, and phones; and generate prefix, phonetic, street-number, street-name, and PO-box keys.

Acceptance: Equivalent business-name and address variants produce comparable matching features; a PO box remains distinct from a physical site.  [Extension; P0]

DQ-02  International data

The solution shall normalize by country and script; retain diacritics, Unicode and original spellings; handle transliteration as additional evidence; support country-aware postal and phone structures, extensions, and timezone-aware dates.

Acceptance: US ZIP plus four and international postal examples preserve format; phone country codes are not inferred solely from a US area-code rule.  [Extension; P0]

DQ-03  Contact normalization

The solution shall parse first, middle, last, preferred and full names, honorifics and suffixes; manage approved nickname evidence; normalize emails and phones conservatively; and classify personal, business, generic and shared contact points.

Acceptance: Role-based emails are not treated as unique people; name variants can become candidates without being automatically declared the same person.  [Extension; P0]

DQ-04  Configurable rules and parity

Administrators shall define ordered rules and derived expressions with syntax validation, sample testing, audit, and execution limits. Distributed and targeted processing shall produce equivalent canonical results for an identical rule version.

Acceptance: The same sample yields identical features through supported engines; unauthorized code execution and uncontrolled expression workloads are prevented.  [Extension; P0]

DQ-05  Quality and freshness scoring

The solution shall score completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, recency and verification at record and attribute level. Domain-specific minimums shall control matching, survivorship, and activation. No missing value shall be fabricated to meet completeness targets.

Acceptance: The UI shows failed checks, score components, and age; invalid high-priority values cannot win over valid eligible alternatives.  [Extension; P0]

DQ-06  Data quality remediation

The solution shall route low-quality data to an owner, display source-level trends, permit corrected resubmission, and distinguish source corrections from governed golden overrides.

Acceptance: A corrected reject is traceable to the original delivery and does not create a second entity or erase the rejection history.  [Extension; P0]

5 Candidate generation and matching

MAT-01  Configurable blocking

The solution shall support field-based and expression-based buckets, required or optional conditions, display order, active flags, boolean gates, and multiple buckets per record. It shall retain candidate-pair origin and deduplicate ordered pairs across buckets.

Acceptance: The same pair is scored once per evaluation version and lists every contributing bucket; null or empty-only keys do not create broad accidental blocks.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-02  Account candidate keys

The solution shall support normalized domain plus postal code with phonetic fallback, phonetic name plus street fragments plus postal code, phonetic name plus postal code, phone plus postal code, ultimate parent plus postal code, and street plus name plus postal code.

Acceptance: The six reference bucket patterns remain configurable; candidates are generated within compatible grains and countries.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-03  Oversized and sparse blocks

Per-bucket block-size and pair-volume limits shall protect capacity. Oversized keys shall be subdivided, handled by a governed fallback, or deferred with measurable coverage loss. Sparse records and no-hit records shall receive secondary retrieval or insufficient-evidence status.

Acceptance: A 50K-record common ZIP never launches an unbounded all-pairs job; unprocessed candidates are visible; source-based Prospect classification shall expose incomplete resolution and shall not imply proven absence of enterprise duplicates.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-04  Deterministic matching

Validated namespaced internal IDs, tax IDs where authorized, DUNS at the correct grain, and approved compound name-address or name-domain conditions shall be configurable. Contradictory strong identifiers shall trigger review or non-match according to precedence.

Acceptance: Equal IDs in unrelated namespaces do not match; exact DUNS does not override grain conflicts or confirmed identifier misuse.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-05  Feature scoring

The solution shall support Levenshtein, no-space comparison, partial ratio, token-sort and token-set comparisons, phonetic and alternate names, address, domain, phone, vendor evidence, and industry context. Feature weights and missing-value treatment shall be explicit.

Acceptance: A score is reproducible, bounded and explainable; enabled weighted features normalize to a documented scale; copying one vendor into another is not counted as independent consensus.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-06  Rule builder

Business users shall configure attribute-to-attribute and attribute-to-literal conditions, equality, similarity thresholds, blank tests, boolean tests, AND or OR groups, scoped gates, source sides, and fixed or either-direction evaluation.

Acceptance: Preview explains each condition and connector; invalid references and contradictory rules cannot be promoted.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-07  Decision thresholds

The solution shall use T_auto and T_review with 0 <= T_review < T_auto <= 1. MATCH_FOUND requires score at least T_auto plus all eligibility gates; CANDIDATES_FOUND lies between thresholds; NO_MATCH lies below T_review after completed evaluation.

Acceptance: Each domain and policy has one approved threshold version; example initial Account values 0.95 and 0.85 remain tunable rather than universal guarantees.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-08  Match outcomes and explanations

Every evaluated candidate shall retain outcome, feature contributions, rule, model and configuration versions, reasons, evidence timestamp, and audit reference. Processing status shall separately identify incomplete or blocked evaluation.

Acceptance: Stewards can explain an automatic match and a rejected candidate; a timeout is never reported as NO_MATCH.  [Baseline; P0]

MAT-09  Matching across time and sources

New and updated records shall match incoming records, current resident source evidence, aliases, and applicable historical identifiers. First-to-first, first-to-third, and third-to-third resolution shall be supported.

Acceptance: A new vendor feed attaches to prior goldens where supported; unchanged old evidence remains available for comparing new records.  [Baseline; P0]

CON-01  Contact candidate generation

The solution shall generate Contact candidates using verified or normalized email plus supporting name, phone plus name, name plus affiliation, source-person IDs, and multilingual name variants. It shall search across employers as well as within an employer.

Acceptance: A changed employer does not automatically create a new person; repeated names at one employer do not automatically merge.  [Extension; P0]

CON-02  Contact identity protection

Matching shall consider verified person IDs, name evidence, email and phone ownership periods, account affiliation, and contradictory identity facts. A shared inbox, switchboard, household phone, or common email domain shall not alone auto-match people.

Acceptance: Two people using purchasing@example.com remain separate; recycled emails and phones trigger temporal checks or review.  [Extension; P0]

CON-03  Person and employment separation

Person identity shall be resolved separately from employer, site, job title and affiliation. The solution shall retain prior employers and simultaneous roles, support contractors and consultants, and assign current-affiliation confidence.

Acceptance: A contact moving from Account A to B keeps their person ID while the old role becomes historical; uncertain employment is not published as verified.  [Extension; P0]

CON-04  Contact usability

A Contact may exist with sparse channels; activation shall require configured identity, validity, current affiliation and permission criteria. Verification, bounce, deceased or inactive flags, and do-not-contact signals shall be preserved independently.

Acceptance: A valid-looking vendor email cannot override an authoritative suppression; matchable contacts with no usable channel stay mastered but ineligible for outreach.  [Extension; P0]

6 Clustering and identity lifecycle

ID-01  Constrained clustering

The solution shall form entities from approved edges while enforcing grain, trusted-identifier and cannot-link constraints across the entire cluster. Connected-component traversal shall not override negative evidence through an intermediate record. User-configured source membership limits apply to the complete prospective cluster and cannot be bypassed by exact scores, fuzzy scores or intermediate records; see section 23.

Acceptance: If A matches B and B resembles C but A and C have a prohibited conflict, the system avoids an unsafe bridge and creates a review case.  [Extension; P0]

ID-02  Stable golden identifiers

The solution shall create opaque persistent golden IDs and reuse historical xrefs for established identities. Source keys shall be scoped by source, entity type and namespace. Singleton eligible records can receive new IDs.

Acceptance: Repeated batches preserve IDs; recycled source IDs create versioned identity mappings rather than hijacking the prior entity.  [Extension; P0]

ID-03  Merge and redirect

When existing identities truly merge, a configurable survivor policy shall retain the established or oldest eligible ID. Retired IDs shall redirect to the survivor and all xrefs, relationships and consumers shall receive the merge event.

Acceptance: Both prior IDs remain resolvable through lineage; the merge preview lists affected accounts, contacts, sites and downstream references.  [Extension; P0]

ID-04  Split and unmerge

Authorized stewards shall reverse erroneous merges or split clusters with evidence, approvals, effective dates and impact analysis. Overrides and cannot-link decisions shall survive subsequent runs unless explicitly superseded.

Acceptance: An unmerge restores correct member assignments and publishes correction events without deleting decision history.  [Extension; P0]

ID-05  Persistent membership and incremental repair

Membership and permitted matching evidence shall include current and historical nondeleted records, not merely the latest batch. Updates and edge removals shall trigger recomputation of impacted components; expanding impact may require a larger rebuild.

Acceptance: Deleting a bridge edge can split the affected entity correctly; untouched components retain IDs and versions.  [Extension; P0]

ID-06  Lifecycle management

Account, organization, site and person states shall include active, inactive, merged, split, review and source-deleted as appropriate, with separate source-based Customer or Prospect classification under CLS rules and separate activity or commercial status attributes.

Acceptance: A source deletion purges that identity under DEL; it does not erase an entity supported by another source; closing a site does not close the entire parent organization.  [Extension; P0]

7 Hierarchies and Account Contact relationships

REL-01  Multiple hierarchy views

The solution shall maintain legal ownership, ultimate parent, operating hierarchy, commercial bill-to and ship-to structure, and channel or CRM structures as distinct effective-dated views. Conflicting vendor hierarchies shall preserve evidence and confidence.

Acceptance: A parent and branch remain distinct identities; the UI shows which hierarchy and source support each edge.  [Extension; P0]

REL-02  Affiliation resolution

Vendor employer IDs, first-party account IDs, location identifiers and corroborating name-domain-address evidence shall link Contacts to organizations, Accounts and sites. Ambiguous or orphan affiliations shall remain unresolved rather than forced.

Acceptance: A contact with two plausible employers is queued or labeled ambiguous; a reliable new employer record resolves the orphan with lineage.  [Extension; P0]

REL-03  Relationship quality

The solution shall enforce valid endpoints, relationship types, temporal consistency and cycle detection in hierarchies intended to be trees. Multiple valid parent views and multiple simultaneous affiliations shall be supported.

Acceptance: Invalid cycles are prevented or quarantined; a merge or split remaps relationships without dangling references.  [Extension; P0]

REL-04  Channel ownership and protection

Channel, account owner, territory, assigned rep and relationship status shall remain first-party business facts. Shared organization identity shall not automatically transfer account ownership or authorize cross-channel activation.

Acceptance: A prospect already owned by another channel is flagged with routing or suppression instead of automatically distributed to a competing sales queue.  [Extension; P0]

8 Survivorship enrichment and golden records

SUR-01  Attribute and group policies

The solution shall support source priority, first eligible non-null waterfall, most recent business timestamp, most frequent value, longest valid value, quality-based selection, and deduplicated aggregation. A configured coherent survivorship group shall select one eligible source row and copy all of its group attributes from that row; independent groups may select different rows. See section 20 for group configuration and decision semantics.

Acceptance: The selected address cannot mix a street from one entity with a city from another; candidate values and every winning reason remain available.  [Extension; P0]

SUR-02  Waterfalls and precedence

Group waterfalls shall support populated anchor fields, required components, most-recent selection and source priorities, with effective dates and deterministic tie-breaking. Field freshness and vendor eligibility shall precede purely cosmetic length.

Acceptance: The reference address waterfall of street populated, postal code populated, then recency can be reproduced and versioned.  [Extension; P0]

SUR-03  First-party authority

Customer and Prospect classification shall use CLS rules; channel ownership and contact permissions shall use designated first-party authority. Vendor enrichment can improve permitted business attributes but shall not overwrite authoritative transaction or permission facts.

Acceptance: A vendor prospect flag cannot override first-party cluster membership; a third-party opt-in does not defeat first-party suppression.  [Extension; P0]

SUR-04  Multiple values and assertions

The solution shall retain alternate names, emails, phones, addresses and provider facts with source, validity, quality, and confidence. Only configured eligible primary values appear as primary golden attributes.

Acceptance: Nondeleted losing values remain traceable and usable as appropriate historical evidence; duplicate aliases are consolidated without losing source contribution.  [Extension; P0]

SUR-05  Overrides and conflicts

Stewards shall approve sensitive values and conflicts, apply reasoned overrides with scope and expiry, and review overrides invalidated by fresh evidence. Industry and revenue disagreements shall be reviewable by policy.

Acceptance: A protected override persists through refresh, expires as configured, and leaves the previous and replacement decisions in audit.  [Extension; P0]

SUR-06  Golden current and history

The solution shall publish current Account and Contact views with source xrefs, member counts, quality, provenance, business effective dates, version, last batch, and timestamps. History shall cover retained nondeleted attributes and relationships; DEL purge requirements override historical retention.

Acceptance: An as-of query reconstructs a previously published value and membership; unchanged values do not create artificial history versions.  [Extension; P0]

SUR-07  Third-party enrichment

The solution shall attach licensed industry, size, revenue, employee, geographic, hierarchy, job role, seniority and validated contact facts at their proper grain. Staleness and source availability shall control selection.

Acceptance: An expired vendor assertion is excluded from future outputs per policy while permitted historical audit remains accessible; conflicting values display their source and age.  [Extension; P0]

9 Customer and Prospect classification

Entity360 classifies each Account and Contact cluster from the provenance of its current contributing members. Customer means at least one first-party source record belongs to the cluster. Prospect means the nonempty cluster contains only third-party source records. No purchase, activity window, CRM lead status, ownership, score or contact permission is required to make this classification. A first-party CRM prospect record therefore makes an Entity360 Customer cluster. The label describes enterprise source presence rather than a claim that a purchase occurred.

Classification applies independently at each mastered grain. A third-party-only Contact associated with a Customer Account remains a Prospect Contact. A first-party Contact does not make its third-party-only employer Account a Customer unless a first-party Account record belongs to that Account cluster. Parent, child and Site relationships do not propagate classification. Match evaluation status and lifecycle remain separate fields and are not additional customer classes.

CLS-01  Source based rule

The solution shall publish Customer when first_party_member_count is greater than zero; otherwise publish Prospect for a nonempty cluster with only third-party members. Source category shall come from an approved source registry, never inferred from a winning attribute.

Acceptance: First-party only and mixed clusters are Customers; third-party-only clusters are Prospects, including singleton clusters.  [Extension; P0]

CLS-02  Domain independence

Account and Contact membership shall be evaluated independently. A linked Account, Site, parent or employer shall not donate its classification to another cluster.

Acceptance: A vendor-only person linked to a CRM Account remains a Prospect Contact; a CRM Contact linked to a vendor-only Account remains a Customer Contact and Prospect Account.  [Extension; P0]

CLS-03  Classification evidence

Goldens, UI, APIs, exports and warehouse views shall expose entity_class, first-party and third-party member counts, contributing sources, rule version, as-of publication and reason.

Acceptance: A user can trace Customer to a specific first-party member even if every surviving display attribute came from a vendor.  [Extension; P0]

CLS-04  Reclassification

Membership changes, merges, splits, confirmed deletions and governed source-category changes shall recompute classification and publish old and new classes with reason and effective time. Only current eligible nondeleted members count.

Acceptance: Adding the first first-party member changes Prospect to Customer without creating a new entity; removing the last first-party member changes Customer to Prospect if third-party members remain.  [Extension; P0]

CLS-05  Lifecycle and history

Inactive but retained contributing first-party members continue to make Customer clusters. Deleted or withdrawn members no longer count, though permitted history retains prior classification. Empty entities shall be retired rather than classified Prospect.

Acceptance: A dormant Customer remains Customer; deleting all members retires the entity; disabling ingestion alone does not remove existing members.  [Extension; P0]

CLS-06  Incomplete processing

Published classification shall describe accepted membership. Match confidence, evaluation completeness, deferred candidates and review status shall be separate. Incomplete retrieval shall not be presented as proof that a Prospect has no enterprise duplicate.

Acceptance: A vendor-only singleton can be published as Prospect with resolution_complete false and held from duplicate-sensitive activation pending resolution.  [Extension; P0]

CLS-07  Governed delivery

Customer and Prospect filters shall operate consistently across all interfaces. Contact usability, permitted purpose, suppression, channel ownership and approval remain independent delivery gates.

Acceptance: A suppressed Customer Contact is not exported for outreach; a Prospect cannot bypass channel or licensing controls.  [Extension; P0]

CLS-08  Classification analytics

Dashboards shall show Customers and Prospects separately for Accounts and Contacts, source overlap and unique contribution, reclassification history, quality, review volumes and approved routing outcomes.

Acceptance: Each metric names its grain, publication and denominator; an affiliation does not double-count a unique person.  [Extension; P0]

10 Stewardship configuration and user experience

UI-01  Review case management

The solution shall queue ambiguous matches, survivorship conflicts, sensitive attributes, data quality, hierarchy conflicts, oversized clusters and affiliation issues. Cases shall show side-by-side evidence, batch, candidate data, assignment, reason, comments and decision.

Acceptance: Cases move through open, in review, approved, rejected or deferred with actor and timestamp; deferred cases remain visible and actionable.  [Baseline; P0]

UI-02  Durable stewardship

Stewards shall approve or reject matches, apply cannot-link and must-link judgments, edit eligible fields, merge, split and escalate. Concurrent decisions shall use conflict detection and approval rules.

Acceptance: A rejected match does not recur as an automatic merge in the next run; simultaneous edits cannot silently overwrite a prior decision.  [Baseline; P0]

UI-03  Configuration administration

Authorized users shall manage sources, mappings, canonical attributes and groups, golden fields, standardization, blocking, algorithms, conditions, weights, thresholds, survivorship and users or roles without direct SQL.

Acceptance: Each screen validates references and inputs, supports preview and governed retirement, and records before and after values.  [Baseline; P0]

UI-04  Configuration release control

Changes shall be versioned, testable, approved and promoted as atomic policy bundles. A running job shall retain its starting version; approved changes apply to the next eligible job. Rollback and impact comparison shall be supported.

Acceptance: A mid-run threshold edit cannot change half a batch; promotion reports expected merges, splits, quality changes and consumer impact.  [Baseline; P0]

UI-05  Monitoring and exploration

The UI shall provide batch history, stage and file drill-down, matching output, blocking-expression tests, source-record search, golden current and history, xrefs, lineage, hierarchy and contact affiliation views, config audit and exports. File and CSV viewers shall obey access policy.

Acceptance: An authorized user traces a golden field to raw source and policy version; large result views use bounded pagination rather than loading the entire dataset.  [Baseline; P0]

UI-06  Usability and access

The UI shall provide filters, understandable error messages, saved views, bulk actions with preview, accessible navigation, and domain-specific permissions. Login and logout shall be supported; reads as well as writes shall be authorized.

Acceptance: A viewer cannot access restricted Contact data by direct URL or export; an analyst only edits permitted pages and domains.  [Baseline; P0]

11 Orchestration publishing and APIs

OPS-01  Pipeline modes

The solution shall support ingest, standardize, block, match, cluster, survive and publish stages; full batch, match-only, create-golden from staged data, targeted rematch, full rebuild, ad hoc test, and controlled reprocess modes.

Acceptance: Match-only stops before golden modification; rematch retains persistent identity and approved stewardship; staging-to-golden uses the specified policy version.  [Extension; P0]

OPS-02  Scheduling and batch control

Runs shall have unique batch IDs with human-readable YYYYMMDD_NNN support, start and end times, config version, record and stage counters, progress and RUNNING, SUCCESS, FAILED or PARTIAL status. Parallel sources shall use globally safe sequencing.

Acceptance: Concurrent workers cannot reuse a run ID; operators can compare runs and identify the exact failed delivery, partition and stage.  [Extension; P0]

OPS-03  Recovery and publish consistency

The solution shall checkpoint partitions, retry transient failures with bounded backoff, quarantine persistent failures, and resume safely. Publication shall expose a complete validated version and prevent mixed golden, xref and relationship states.

Acceptance: Fault injection before and after a write yields no lost or duplicate logical records; consumers stay on the prior valid publication until the new version commits.  [Extension; P0]

OPS-04  Future external read-only match API

In a later release, the solution shall preserve Account lookup behavior and add Contact lookup with source identity, domain-specific attributes, return-candidate flag and bounded candidate limit. Responses shall include MATCH_FOUND, CANDIDATES_FOUND or NO_MATCH, golden ID and record, scores, reasons, features and audit ID.

Acceptance: A lookup makes no ingestion or golden changes; invalid input is rejected; dependencies unavailable return a processing error rather than a false non-match.  [Extension; P1]

OPS-05  Internal exploration and future consumer APIs

Internal application screens shall support governed exploration. External consumers in a later release shall query by golden or source ID, search exact and fuzzy fields, retrieve history, xrefs, hierarchy and affiliations, and receive permission-filtered output. Health and readiness endpoints shall be available.

Acceptance: Retired golden IDs redirect predictably; pagination and rate limits apply; sensitive features and evidence are masked according to authorization.  [Extension; P0]

OPS-06  Bulk and delta publishing

The solution shall publish warehouse views and approved CSV or Parquet extracts; external API JSON delivery is deferred. It shall publish, full snapshots and changed-record deltas with schema and publication version, checksums and control totals. Merge, split, delete and suppression corrections shall be explicit events.

Acceptance: A consumer replays a delta without double application and reconciles it to a full snapshot at the same watermark.  [Extension; P0]

OPS-07  Consumer contracts

Output contracts shall define entity grain, null semantics, datatype, ID and alias behavior, freshness, schema evolution, acknowledgment and replay. Business data access shall be consistent across UI, API, export and warehouse.

Acceptance: A consumer cannot recover prohibited vendor fields through an alternate interface; schema-breaking changes require controlled version adoption.  [Extension; P0]

12 Security governance and audit

GOV-01  Roles and separation of duties

The solution shall retain Administrator, Analyst and Viewer roles with configurable page edit permissions and add Steward, Approver, Operator, Consumer and Auditor capabilities. Object, field, domain and export access shall be configurable.

Acceptance: Tests cover positive and negative UI and API access; high-impact policy changes and bulk merges have independent approval where configured.  [Extension; P0]

GOV-02  Authentication and secrets

Production shall support enterprise SSO, managed user lifecycle, service identities, session expiry, and organization-mandated authentication controls. Credentials shall be managed outside code; any retained local login shall use strong password hashing.

Acceptance: Deactivated identities lose access; demo or seeded users are disabled or replaced; no source or platform secret appears in logs or exports.  [Extension; P0]

GOV-03  Protection and entitlements

The solution shall encrypt transit and storage, mask restricted personal and commercial data, enforce vendor field-level permitted use and time limits, and audit exports. Contact permission is purpose, channel, source and jurisdiction scoped.

Acceptance: Vendor expiry or suppression blocks new activation and restricted export; golden merges cannot broaden the permission of any contact point.  [Extension; P0]

GOV-04  Deletion retention and restoration

Approved retention and deletion policies shall cover raw, stage, goldens, histories, candidate evidence, caches, search indexes, exports and backups. Legal holds and minimal non-sensitive audit evidence shall follow approved policy. Restores must reapply tombstones.

Acceptance: A deletion test reconciles all active surfaces and emits consumer corrections; restoring a backup cannot resurrect suppressed personal data for activation.  [Extension; P0]

GOV-05  Decision lineage and replay

The solution shall record input snapshot or immutable reference, blocking rule, features, match and cluster decision, survivorship candidates and winner, batch, model, configuration, actor and time. Configuration audit shall record old and new values and pending, approved, rejected or waived status.

Acceptance: A historical decision can be reconstructed within its retained evidence window; nondeterministic or external-model dependencies disclose replay limits.  [Extension; P0]

GOV-06  Data governance ownership

Each source, golden domain, hierarchy, critical attribute and classification policy shall have a business owner and steward. Quality incidents, disputed values, and policy exceptions shall have documented escalation and resolution.

Acceptance: Production onboarding cannot complete without ownership, acceptance rules, source agreements and operating procedures.  [Extension; P0]

13 Capacity performance and resilience

The capacity requirement is 1.5 billion simultaneously resident logical source records across Accounts and Contacts, including the evidence needed to match incremental changes. It does not mean 1.5 billion golden entities or 1.5 billion newly loaded records every night. Historical versions, raw payloads, relationship edges, candidate pairs, audit records and indexes are additional storage and processing loads and must be sized separately. An approved workload must state the Account versus Contact mix and source-record-to-golden ratio.

At 1.5B records, all-pairs comparison would produce approximately 1.125 quintillion pairs. Bounded candidate retrieval, distributed execution and controlled affected-component processing are therefore release requirements. The 10M-record targets in the references cannot be extrapolated into a proven 1.5B service level.

SCALE-01  Resident-scale operation

Ingestion, candidate retrieval, matching, clustering, survivorship, history, browsing and lookup shall work against the 1.5B resident-record footprint without moving the corpus into a single process. IDs, counters and partition offsets shall support values above 32-bit limits.

Acceptance: A full-scale benchmark validates end-to-end correctness, lookup and delta processing with representative skew, sparsity, source multiplicity and Contact affiliations.  [Extension; P0]

SCALE-02  Distributed processing

The production data plane shall use partitioned distributed or warehouse-native processing, bulk reads and writes, locality-aware retrieval, spill control, checkpointing and independent worker scaling. Per-record remote ORM operations shall not be the bulk path.

Acceptance: A lost worker resumes from committed checkpoints; hot partitions do not exhaust driver memory; throughput and resource costs are reported per stage.  [Extension; P0]

SCALE-03  Bounded candidate cost

Candidate budgets, block subdivision, cross-partition resolution and deferred-work queues shall balance cost and recall. No arbitrary top-K cutoff shall silently discard valid match evidence. Candidate counts and recall loss shall be measurable.

Acceptance: Load tests include common domains, switchboards and huge parent families; fallback coverage and cap-hit counts are visible.  [Extension; P0]

SCALE-04  Incremental graph scope

Updates shall compare against the resident corpus and re-evaluate affected graph neighborhoods, survivorship, hierarchy, affiliations and Customer or Prospect classification. Broad rule changes shall use controlled backfills or shadow rebuilds.

Acceptance: A delta that touches an existing large component expands processing correctly or defers explicitly; a nightly delta is not a mandatory full-history scan.  [Extension; P0]

SCALE-05  Highly available control plane

Configuration, security, run state, metrics and review decisions shall use an enterprise transactional store or equivalent service with concurrency, backup and failover. SQLite may remain for development or isolated deployments.

Acceptance: Concurrent policy edits and worker checkpoints retain consistency during failover; the control plane is not a single local-file production dependency.  [Extension; P0]

SCALE-06  Cost and retention controls

The solution shall expose compute, storage, index, model and vendor-service usage by source, stage and run. Evidence retention, cold storage and sampled operational telemetry shall be configurable without losing required decision lineage.

Acceptance: Budgets trigger alerts or controlled throttling; a 1.5B sizing model includes raw, canonical, golden, history, edges, audit and backup multipliers.  [Extension; P0]

SCALE-07  Availability and disaster recovery

The solution shall isolate batch and interactive workloads, back up control and operational state, and prove coordinated recovery of published data and identity. Proposed online availability is 99.9 percent monthly; proposed RPO is 15 minutes for accepted transactional changes and RTO is four hours.

Acceptance: A recovery exercise measures achieved RPO and RTO; replay restores the correct publication, xrefs and suppressions without duplicate identity.  [Extension; P0]

13 2 Benchmark evidence and release gates

The benchmark shall record dataset sizes by domain, unique entities, source count, change rate, record width, average and tail candidates per changed record, giant-component distribution, concurrency, infrastructure, run settings, and total cost. Test broad business-name variation, common personal names, international data, very large hierarchies, shared contact points, stale evidence, deletion and replay. Include cold and warm lookup behavior and concurrent batch processing.

Quality tests shall use independently adjudicated labels and untouched holdouts stratified by domain, source pair, country, sparse data and difficult cases. Measure candidate recall separately from scoring recall, and pair accuracy separately from cluster over-merge and split error. State sample sizes, class imbalance and uncertainty. Validate person identity and affiliation accuracy independently. A headline average cannot hide an unacceptable high-risk segment.

Scale acceptance requires count reconciliation, stable IDs, retained constraints, correct merges and splits, fault recovery, publish atomicity, suppression enforcement, and repeatable cost reporting at the full footprint. Passing a smaller test or storing 1.5B rows alone is insufficient. Full-scale run duration and budget must be approved before production commitment.

15 Business scenario and acceptance catalog

The catalog defines the principal end-to-end, exception and boundary scenarios for UAT. Each scenario shall be exercised with source-specific variants and the same policy versions used for production. It supplements the acceptance conditions attached to individual requirements.

SC-01  New organization across sources

Trigger: Salesforce, D&B and billing name or address variants. Expected result: One organization at the agreed grain; retain every source xref and explain surviving attributes.

SC-02  Unchanged delivery replay

Trigger: Same file delivered twice. Expected result: No duplicate active records or duplicate consumer events; delivery and duplicate counts reconcile.

SC-03  Equal payload different sources

Trigger: Two source IDs have exactly equal values. Expected result: Preserve both source identities; resolve their entity through matching, not destructive hash suppression.

SC-04  Name variation

Trigger: Acme Inc and Acmee Incorporated with corroborating location. Expected result: Generate candidates; configurable phonetic and string features support review or approved match.

SC-05  Revenue conflict

Trigger: First-party and vendor revenue bands disagree. Expected result: Apply configured eligibility and recency policy; show both assertions and winning reason.

SC-06  Ambiguous Account pair

Trigger: Similar name and shared domain but conflicting evidence. Expected result: Queue review within configured threshold band; never auto-merge solely on a domain.

SC-07  New source onboarding

Trigger: Acquired CRM delivers 50K records. Expected result: Validate mappings, compare with resident evidence, reuse justified IDs, and quarantine bad records.

SC-08  Threshold change

Trigger: Engineer adjusts weights and thresholds. Expected result: Shadow comparison exposes merge and split impact; one approved bundle applies to each run.

SC-09  Trusted identifier conflict

Trigger: Equal DUNS but incompatible entity grain or trusted facts. Expected result: Review or reject according to precedence; do not blindly match regardless of contradictions.

SC-10  Identifier collision

Trigger: Same numeric source ID in two systems. Expected result: Keep namespaces separate; no deterministic collision.

SC-11  Same name different companies

Trigger: ABC Services in two countries or addresses. Expected result: Retain distinct identities unless sufficient positive evidence establishes sameness.

SC-12  Shared corporate domain

Trigger: Parent and several branches share website. Expected result: Link hierarchy; retain distinct organization or site identities.

SC-13  Co-located businesses

Trigger: Several businesses share building, suite or coworking address. Expected result: Use identity evidence and unit detail; address similarity alone is insufficient.

SC-14  PO box and physical address

Trigger: Business has mailing box and office address. Expected result: Retain typed addresses; name plus ZIP alone cannot prove the same site.

SC-15  Missing address phone rule

Trigger: Name plus phone with blank address. Expected result: Evaluate approved compound rule and shared-phone risk; record blank semantics accurately.

SC-16  Shared ultimate parent

Trigger: Subsidiaries share parent and ZIP. Expected result: Form hierarchy candidates; do not collapse distinct subsidiaries automatically.

SC-17  SIC or NAICS equality

Trigger: Two businesses have same industry code. Expected result: Use industry as supporting context, never a unique identity key.

SC-18  No blocking hit

Trigger: Sparse account lacks useful retrieval keys. Expected result: Flag insufficient evidence or use fallback; classify from current source membership and expose incomplete resolution separately.

SC-19  Oversized block

Trigger: 50K rows share ZIP or switchboard. Expected result: Subdivide or defer visibly; preserve coverage accounting and workload limits.

SC-20  Unsafe bridge

Trigger: A matches B, B matches C, A conflicts with C. Expected result: Prevent prohibited transitive merge; explain conflict and queue review.

SC-21  Historical ID reuse

Trigger: Existing source record refreshed next month. Expected result: Preserve golden ID and effective-dated source mapping.

SC-22  Two goldens truly bridge

Trigger: Fresh evidence establishes same entity. Expected result: Retain approved survivor ID, redirect retired ID, and publish merge correction.

SC-23  Erroneous merge reversal

Trigger: Steward identifies two separate companies or people. Expected result: Split or unmerge; preserve audit, redirects and corrected consumer references.

SC-24  Legal acquisition

Trigger: Company acquired but remains legally separate. Expected result: Update dated ownership edges; merge only when identity itself changes.

SC-25  Site move or closure

Trigger: Business relocates or closes one branch. Expected result: Apply site lifecycle policy, retain address history and avoid closing its parent.

SC-26  Full snapshot missing rows

Trigger: Vendor refresh is incomplete. Expected result: Quarantine snapshot or apply completeness policy; do not mass-delete from unexplained omission.

SC-27  Source delete with other support

Trigger: Vendor removes record still present in CRM. Expected result: Deactivate vendor contribution; retain entity from remaining support and recompute survivor.

SC-28  Late stale update

Trigger: Older delivery arrives after newer record. Expected result: Preserve history; do not overwrite newer current facts.

SC-29  Recycled source ID

Trigger: CRM reuses a retired record key. Expected result: Disambiguate identity version and avoid attaching a new person to the retired entity.

SC-30  Grouped address selection

Trigger: One source has street only, another has complete address. Expected result: Select an eligible coherent group and log waterfall and losing candidates.

SC-31  Steward override refresh

Trigger: Vendor feed contradicts approved override. Expected result: Honor scope and expiry, or raise conflict; never silently erase the override.

SC-32  One person multiple vendors

Trigger: Same person appears in CRM and two contact providers. Expected result: Create one Contact when supported; retain distinct evidence and contact points.

SC-33  Same name same employer

Trigger: Two employees named John Smith. Expected result: Remain separate absent stronger identity evidence; queue uncertainty.

SC-34  Nickname and transliteration

Trigger: Liz and Elizabeth or script variants. Expected result: Use approved evidence and corroboration; preserve original name forms.

SC-35  Job change

Trigger: Contact moves from Account A to B. Expected result: Preserve person ID, end or qualify prior affiliation, and establish dated new affiliation.

SC-36  Multiple current roles

Trigger: Consultant serves two organizations. Expected result: Preserve simultaneous affiliations and role-specific contact channels.

SC-37  Shared role inbox

Trigger: Several people use purchasing@example.com. Expected result: Classify shared channel; do not merge people or infer a named person.

SC-38  Recycled email or phone

Trigger: A former employee channel is reassigned. Expected result: Check ownership dates and conflicting names; avoid false person merge.

SC-39  Personal email employer

Trigger: Contact uses consumer email domain. Expected result: Do not infer employer from the domain; require independent affiliation evidence.

SC-40  Orphan vendor contact

Trigger: Employer account unresolved. Expected result: Master eligible person but hold affiliation and activation pending evidence.

SC-41  Contact suppression

Trigger: Vendor email is valid but CRM says do not contact. Expected result: Retain suppressed point where permitted; block outreach and activation exports.

SC-42  No usable channel

Trigger: Person known but emails invalid or absent. Expected result: Retain Contact; report missing channel separately from missing person.

SC-43  Third-party Account Prospect

Trigger: Account cluster contains only third-party members. Expected result: Classify Prospect and expose membership evidence and resolution completeness; apply delivery gates separately.

SC-44  Independent Site classification

SC-45  Third-party Contact Prospect

SC-46  Persona gap

Trigger: Known account has finance contacts but no IT buyer. Expected result: Report role coverage gap without fabricating a person or double-counting known Contacts.

SC-47  Dormant customer

SC-48  Cross-channel conflict

SC-49  Prospect becomes Customer

SC-50  Vendor overlap

SC-51  Vendor rights expiry

SC-52  Match-only run

Trigger: User tests candidate outcomes. Expected result: Expose output and metrics without changing current goldens.

SC-53  Create golden from staging

Trigger: Approved matching exists in staging. Expected result: Create using explicit policy snapshot and validate xrefs before publication.

SC-54  Worker or warehouse failure

Trigger: Connection fails mid-stage. Expected result: Retry or resume from checkpoint; no mixed publication or lost logical record.

SC-55  API no match versus error

Trigger: Lookup cannot reach candidate service. Expected result: Return error and audit correlation; never misreport completed NO_MATCH.

SC-56  Concurrent stewardship

Trigger: Two users edit same review case. Expected result: Detect stale version, preserve decisions, and require resolution.

SC-57  Unauthorized export

SC-58  1.5B footprint delta

SC-59  Deletion and restore

SC-63  Split changes class

SC-64  First-party only cluster

SC-70  Global base directory

Trigger: Admin configures base directory. Expected result: Validate canonical location and permissions; retain revision and audit.

16 Logical data requirements and reference compatibility

The physical schema is an architecture deliverable. The business model shall preserve the meaning and lineage of the supplied operational and metadata structures while extending them for Contact, Site, organization, affiliation, temporal evidence, entitlements and published versions. Referenced code paths describe the supplied design, not independently verified production behavior.

Logical object

Minimum content and reference alignment

Source registry and run state

Source definitions, delivery versions, run status, stage metrics and ownership; aligns to mdm_source_system, mdm_batch_run and mdm_batch_level_metric.

Canonical source evidence

Raw reference, canonical attributes, derived values, quality, event and ingestion dates; extends mdm_source_record_stg to all entity types.

Candidates and match evidence

Pair endpoints, bucket and key, score, reasons, feature values, decision and policy version; aligns to mdm_blocking_result and mdm_match_edge. Include decision coverage beyond only positive edges.

Membership and identity

Entity grain, persistent IDs, source xrefs, active flags, membership history, merge redirects and split events; extends mdm_cluster and mdm_account_xref with Contact and Site counterparts.

Current and historical goldens

Complete domain attributes, versions and effective dates; extends mdm_golden_account_current and mdm_golden_account_history with Contact, organization and Site views.

Attribute assertions and decisions

Candidate and winning values, source record, group, rule, confidence and timestamps; aligns to mdm_golden_attribute_audit.

Review and duplicates

Case reason, side-by-side evidence, status, assignee, decision, comments and resolution; duplicate hash, original, count, first and last seen; extends mdm_review_queue and mdm_duplicate_record.

Relationships and classification

Dated hierarchy and affiliations, ownership, confidence, Customer or Prospect, member counts, class reason, resolution completeness and delivery state; new logical objects.

Dictionary and mapping metadata

Attribute and group definitions, types, storage formats, roles, cardinality, samples, transforms and date formats; retains mdm_canonical_attribute_group, mdm_canonical_attribute and mdm_source_attribute_mapping.

Rule metadata

16 2 Reference issues resolved in the requirements

Threshold terminology is inconsistent: the business reference proposes 0.85 automatic matching and 0.70 review, while the technical reference and seed propose 0.95 automatic matching and 0.80 or 0.85 review. MAT-07 replaces these with explicit T_auto and T_review semantics. Values remain domain-specific starting configurations, subject to validation.

The reference duplicate scenario conflates normalized similarity with exact content duplication. ING-06 retains separate source identities, while matching resolves equivalent records. Likewise, a SIC code or ultimate parent is not a unique business identity; same-parent and PO-box seed rules become guarded evidence under MAT and REL policies. Equal DUNS does not universally justify ignoring contradictory identity or grain.

The reference constraints of organization-only, batch-only, and read-only source integration are superseded by Account and Contact domain coverage and the phased ingestion and writeback recommendations. The reference Spark and Snowflake separation remains a viable design input, but in-memory global blocking indexes or union-find, single-file SQLite control state, and destructive full rematch are not acceptable default production strategies at this footprint.

Compatibility migration shall preserve existing spelling aliases such as phoentic_business_name and postal variants zip_code_9 and zip_supp or ZIP plus four mappings until consumers transition. The technical prose also describes one physical postal suffix inconsistently; mapping must be verified against actual schema before migration. New business definitions shall use consistent names without silently breaking existing metadata.

Snowflake standard-table primary-key, unique and foreign-key declarations do not enforce these integrity guarantees; see the technical design references. The solution must explicitly validate source-key uniqueness, member assignments, golden versions and relationship endpoints before publication. Database capabilities and supported DDL must be verified in the implementation environment.

20 3 Group survivorship and controlled activation

WF-08  Coherent group membership and anchor

Administrators shall define survivorship group name, required anchor attribute and members selected from golden-eligible attributes. Each attribute shall belong to no more than one active survivorship group for a domain and release. A group selects one winning source row, with all grouped values taken from that row.

Acceptance: An attribute cannot join two active groups; an anchor outside the group or eligible set is rejected. The business name and its grouped expression come from one row. An address cannot mix components from competing rows, including to fill a null.

WF-09  Ordered sources and waterfall steps

Acceptance: Saving is atomic across group members, anchor, sources and steps. The saved order is reproduced on reload. WF-10  Preview and versioned rule activation

Configuration changes shall validate cross-references and preview affected groups, golden records, classifications and downstream outputs before activation. Runs shall pin an approved immutable configuration revision. A saved draft shall not silently change a running batch.

Acceptance: A concurrent edit raises a revision conflict; an invalid rule never activates. Approved changes trigger an affected-record backfill or governed rebuild. Rollback selects an earlier policy and schedules corrective processing; it does not erase later publications without reconciliation.

20 4 Processing exploration and controlled access

Acceptance: No input silently disappears. Accepted, rejected, unchanged, deleted and deferred counts reconcile under a declared counting contract. A deleted bridge causes a split when needed; stale replay cannot reinstate deleted evidence.

WF-12  Administration audit and safe file inspection

User and role administration, configuration audit and file/CSV inspection shall be governed functions. File inspection shall be restricted to authorized configured locations and bounded previews, with sensitive values protected and deleted payloads removed under DEL.

Within a policy, users shall configure attribute mappings, exact equality or similarity algorithm, tokenizer, threshold, AND/OR groups, direction and active state. Different attributes and source pairs may use different thresholds. Both canonical and ingestion-derived attributes are selectable.

Acceptance: A configured MDR-to-MDR policy requires school name and address equality, while separately configured MDR-to-DNB and MDR-to-Salesforce policies permit chosen fuzzy comparisons. These source names and attributes are examples only. Preview shows the effective policy for either record order.

SP-02  Mandatory gates and policy precedence

Users shall distinguish mandatory source-scoped conditions from alternative qualifying clauses. For example, exact school-name and address requirements for a same-source pair shall be enforced as mandatory gates across all applicable match paths. Configuration shall define source scope, precedence and fallback behavior for overlapping specific, source-category and general rules.

Acceptance: A global fuzzy rule or another OR clause cannot bypass the applicable mandatory equality gates. Conflicting equal-precedence policies are rejected or require an explicit resolution. No applicable policy is visible as an unresolved policy case, with no implicit automatic match.

23 2 Configurable cluster cardinality

SP-03  Source membership constraints

Users shall configure a maximum number of distinct current source records allowed in a resulting cluster, scoped by source and entity domain. Support a conditional rule of the form if the prospective cluster satisfies a configured source-presence condition, then a configured target source has at most N records. A source can be both trigger and target. The MDR example sets the MDR limit to one.

Acceptance: Two current logical MDR records cannot coexist in a cluster governed by limit one, even if their attributes match exactly. Versions of the same source record do not increase the count. No source limit exists unless configured; engine resource bounds remain separately governed.

SP-04  Enforcement across the entire resulting cluster

Validate the complete prospective membership before accepting a merge, assigning a new record, activating an incremental repair or publishing a rebuilt cluster. Limits shall apply to indirect paths through other sources as well as direct same-source pairs. A steward action cannot override a configured hard limit without an authorized policy change and revalidation.

Acceptance: If MDR-A matches DNB-B and DNB-B matches MDR-C, the system prevents a resulting cluster containing MDR-A and MDR-C when the MDR limit is one. DNB-B cannot bridge around the constraint. Concurrent processing cannot publish a violating cluster.

SP-05  Configuration impact and operational explanation

Users shall create, preview, approve, activate and audit versioned source-pair policies and membership constraints. A rule change shall identify affected pairs and clusters, including resident data, and schedule re-evaluation and repair. Conflict handling, review routing and deterministic selection policy shall be configured or approved and visible.

Acceptance: Reducing a source limit causes existing violating clusters to be repaired or held from new publication under a declared transition policy. Explanations identify the constraint, source count, limit, affected identities, policy revision and blocked merge. Matching success and cluster admission are reported separately.

Configuration scope

User configured behavior

MDR versus MDR

Resulting cluster

When MDR is present, maximum distinct current MDR records = 1. Check the entire prospective cluster.

Other sources and attributes

Use their own configured policies and limits. No MDR settings, fuzzy percentages or education formulas are platform defaults.



With a limit of one, even two exactly matching MDR records cannot join the same cluster as distinct current members. A second delivery of the same source ID is an update or replay handled by ingestion, not a second logical member. Consolidating distinct source IDs before mastering is not assumed and cannot silently circumvent the limit. If same-source exact matches should actually coexist, the user must configure a different limit or a separately approved record-consolidation policy.

Acceptance cases include: one MDR record with fuzzy-matched DNB and Salesforce records is allowed when their pair policies and other gates pass; two MDR records connected through one or multiple third-source records are blocked from the same cluster; no configured source limit means no business cardinality restriction for that source; deleting a constrained-source member purges its evidence and repairs the cluster; adding a new user-defined derived field enables configured rule use without a software change. Entity domain and exact-comparison normalization are selected during configuration. Clause operands and null checks are entered by the user; no fixed Clause 4 behavior is required.
